A B2B PPC Change Log Should Preserve the Question Behind Each Edit

Megan Collins · 27 October 2026

A paid-search account can contain a long history of edits without a clear record of what the team was trying to learn. A keyword was paused, a form was shortened and a landing-page message was revised. Later, someone sees a different cost per enquiry and tries to explain it. The edit history shows what changed, but it rarely provides the reasoning needed to interpret the result.

A useful campaign change log connects each decision to a question about the enquiry journey. It does not need to become another large reporting system. It needs enough context for a colleague to understand the concern, the agreed response and the evidence that will inform a later review. Keep sensitive contact information in the organisation's authorised systems rather than reproducing it in the log.

Start with an observation rather than a verdict

Describe what the team actually noticed. Perhaps several recent enquiries asked for something the offer did not provide. Perhaps assessments are still pending, or a tracking event appears to fire twice. These observations require different investigations. Calling all of them a lead-quality problem hides the distinction before the review has begun.

Record where the observation came from and what remains uncertain. An isolated conversation can be a useful prompt, but it does not establish how the whole campaign behaves. Check whether the records being compared refer to the same period, offer and stage of assessment. This helps the team avoid changing an advertisement to solve a problem in a receiving process.

Write the question the change is meant to answer

Turn the concern into a specific question. Does the page explain who the offer is for? Can the receiving person identify what the visitor requested? Are repeated submissions being counted as separate opportunities? A precise question makes the proposed change easier to discuss and gives the later review a practical purpose.

DemandTech's B2B PPC strategy guide considers enquiry quality alongside spend. Use that wider perspective when choosing the question. A lower cost for a form completion does not by itself explain whether the resulting request is relevant or whether someone has assessed it.

Keep the decision and its limits together

Describe the agreed edit in plain language and identify who owns it. Note the affected campaign or page using the team's normal internal references. Explain what the edit is expected to clarify, without presenting an expected improvement as a guaranteed outcome. Keep the record proportionate to the decision.

If several changes must happen together, say so. A coordinated offer revision may require changes to the advertisement, landing page and confirmation message. The log should acknowledge that combination so the next reviewer does not attribute every subsequent difference to one small edit. Where practical, avoid adding unrelated changes during the same assessment period.

Review after the relevant evidence is available

Choose a review point that fits the decision and the available evidence. Enquiries may need time for assessment, while a broken confirmation can be checked directly. Do not impose an arbitrary waiting period on every kind of issue. Record why the chosen review point is useful and who will gather the information.

Broader B2B marketing planning helps connect that review to the purpose of the offer. If a resource is intended to support early research, judge it against that role rather than treating every download as an immediate buying conversation. Separate operational checks from conclusions about commercial value.

Record the next decision without rewriting history

When the review happens, add what was observed and what the team decided next. Keep the original question visible even if the answer is inconclusive. A clear record that more assessment is needed is more useful than a confident explanation built from incomplete information.

Where appropriate, B2B lead nurturing can support relevant interest after the first response. Keep those follow-up decisions aligned with the person's request and permission. End each change-log entry with an owner and a next step. Over time, the log becomes a record of considered decisions rather than a list of unexplained campaign adjustments.

Before closing the entry, ask a colleague whether they could explain the decision without attending the original meeting. If they cannot, add the missing context rather than more reporting fields. A short explanation of the offer, assessment stage or practical constraint often prevents the same discussion from starting again when campaign ownership changes.
